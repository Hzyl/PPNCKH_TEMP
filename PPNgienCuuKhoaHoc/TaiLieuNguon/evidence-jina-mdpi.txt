Chikungunya is a mosquito-borne viral disease and an important public health challenge, particularly in regions where competent Aedes vectors are established. High-quality question-answering resources grounded in authoritative clinical guidance are needed to support the development and evaluation of Artificial Intelligence (AI) applications for this disease. ChikungunyaQA is a Brazilian Portuguese (PT-BR) question-answering dataset designed to support the development and evaluation of language-model applications grounded in authoritative Chikungunya guidance. The dataset was constructed from six Brazilian clinical and technical documents, which were semantically segmented and enriched with provenance and clinical metadata. GPT-4o mini generated multi-persona question–answer pairs for physicians, patients, and caregivers using an inductive, source-grounded pipeline. All instances were independently evaluated with Claude Sonnet 4.5 using G-Eval across faithfulness, completeness, persona appropriateness, and clinical relevance, then pairs below a threshold underwent feedback-guided repair and re-evaluation. The final dataset contains 1262 retained QA pairs generated from 112 productive source fragments, among 141 fragments extracted from six documents. Mean G-Eval score was 97.12/100, with 99.7% of generated pairs retained. Human evaluation of 200 pairs by a physician and a physical therapist showed high ratings for clinical accuracy, completeness, and persona adherence, with Gwet’s AC2 ranging from 0.944 to 0.961 in the shared subset. ChikungunyaQA provides a reusable resource intended to support research on fine-tuning and evaluating language models, developing RAG-based systems, and investigating factual consistency and hallucinations in healthcare AI.

Small tabular datasets can produce uncertain performance estimates because limited sample information affects both model fitting and evaluation. This study developed a procedure calibrated through simulation to estimate the mean absolute discrepancy between performance on out-of-bag observations and performance on an independent reference sample across models fitted to bootstrap samples. The procedure uses quantities that can be observed during bootstrap evaluation. The primary experiment included 60 simulation scenarios, 3000 independent small datasets, four classification algorithms, and 100 bootstrap repetitions per dataset. Large independent reference samples were used during simulation to define the calibration targets. Gradient Boosting calibrators were developed using 40 scenarios and evaluated on 20 reserved scenarios. In reserved evaluation, AUC error estimation achieved an MAE of 0.0164, an $R^{2}$ of 0.7217, and a Spearman correlation of 0.8855. For Brier Score error, the corresponding values were 0.0090, 0.7848, and 0.9143. The complete calibration model also showed better error estimation than reduced configurations based on bootstrap variability alone. Under the three alternative data-generating mechanisms examined, the calibration models maintained similar performance, while Monte Carlo assessment showed small uncertainty in the main stress-test estimates. A methodological case study with 50 participants illustrated application of the procedure when independent reference performance was unavailable. The procedure complements conventional AUC and Brier Score reporting by estimating the mean absolute discrepancy between out-of-bag and independent reference performance from information available in a small observed dataset.

The widespread adoption of smart TVs has made content overload in television programming increasingly severe, and high-quality recommender systems are critical for improving user experience. However, the broadcast-television-recommendation field has long lacked large-scale public datasets that realistically reflect its scenario-specific characteristics, which has constrained the development and validation of recommendation algorithms for broadcast television. To address this gap, this paper constructs and releases BroadCastRec, a large-scale real-world dataset designed specifically for broadcast television recommendation. The dataset is derived from 12 months of IPTV viewing records from a province in China. After data cleaning and anonymization, it contains more than 360,000 users, 44,000 programs, and 113 million user–program interaction records. BroadCastRec provides timestamps, user payment-tier information, and rich program metadata, thereby supporting large-scale program-level analysis of television viewing while leaving complete channel-level EPG reconstruction to a future release. Finally, experiments on benchmark models for click-through rate prediction, sequential recommendation, and multi-task learning show that existing models achieve substantially lower performance on BroadCastRec than on general-domain datasets. These results not only confirm the unique challenges of broadcast television recommendation, but also demonstrate the reliability of BroadCastRec as a new benchmark dataset.

Reliable urban air-quality assessment increasingly depends on integrating heterogeneous data sources, yet doing so in data-sparse cities, where reference monitoring is limited and low-cost sensors dominate, remains an open challenge. This study presents a reproducible framework for integrating and analyzing heterogeneous urban sensor data, demonstrated on Almaty, Kazakhstan. Two complementary sources were linked at the station level through a shared identifier: a historical low-cost network of 146 stations (2020–2026; 544,943 records) and a real-time collector providing traffic and source-context features. A three-stage quality-control pipeline retained 442,322 records across 39 stations. Spatial inequality was quantified with complementary indices, attributed to emission sources through geometric descriptors, and used to compare predictive models. Exposure was substantially unequal (winter, seasonally standardized Gini 0.197; full-period Gini 0.210; population-weighted Gini 0.09), with the population-weighted annual mean PM2.5 (19.8 µg/m 3) nearly four times the WHO annual guideline (5 µg/m 3); virtually the entire population lives in districts whose annual mean exceeds this guideline. The inequality showed a spatial association with position downwind (_p_ = 0.056), reported as exploratory given Almaty’s lack of a consistent prevailing winter wind. On the sparse network, regression failed to predict continuous concentrations (R 2 ≈ 0), and once evaluation leakage was removed, classification of high-exposure locations did not exceed a prevalence-aware baseline either (best model 72%, 95% CI overlapping the baseline), indicating that neither task is reliably validated at this network size—a cautionary finding for data-sparse settings generally. The framework provides a transferable template for air-quality assessment in data-scarce urban environments.
